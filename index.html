Online Safety Checklist

Strong passwords, sharp eyes — stay safe online

Why this matters

Online safety is not a theory subject — it is the first skill every job seeker needs. A fake "shortlisted" message, a form asking for a fee, a weak password: one careless click can cost money or an account. This page was built as a companion to VU's free Digital Awareness course (PTC801, 4 weeks), which teaches exactly these skills — strong passwords, spotting phishing, and managing your personal data. Try the tools below, tick the habits, and keep them.

Privacy note: everything here runs in your browser. Nothing you type or tick is sent anywhere. And never type a real password into a demo — test with a made-up one.

1. Password strength checker

Type a made-up password to test it. A strong password is long and mixed — and different for every site.

Not tested
  • At least 12 characters
  • At least one capital letter (A–Z)
  • At least one small letter (a–z)
  • At least one number (0–9)
  • At least one symbol (! @ # $ %)
  • Not a common password like "password123" or "123456"

2. Phishing red-flag checklist

Got a "shortlisted" message or a job form? Tick every red flag you can see. The risk score tells you how careful to be.

0 red flags ticked — check the message carefully.

If a form asks for a fee, your CNIC or bank details before you join, it is not a job — it is a scam. Pay nothing, send nothing, and ask someone you trust first.

4. Safe habits

Important note

This is a demo learning tool. It cannot guarantee that a message is safe or a scam — when in doubt, verify on the official website or ask someone you trust, and never pay a fee, CNIC number or bank details to an unknown sender.
